Frequently Asked Questions
How does the calorie calculator work?

The calculator works in three simple steps. First, you fill out the form – you enter your gender, age, height, weight, physical activity level and your goal (cut, maintenance, bulk or recomposition). Then you click "Calculate requirements". The calculator instantly determines your BMR using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, multiplies it by your activity coefficient and displays your TDEE. Based on this, you receive three calorie targets – deficit, maintenance and surplus – as well as a macronutrient breakdown (protein, carbohydrates, fats) tailored to your chosen goal. All calculations are performed locally in your browser – no data is sent to any server.

What is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your total daily calorie requirement – the number of calories your body burns throughout the day, taking your physical activity level into account. It includes your basal metabolic rate (BMR) as well as calories burned during exercise, digestion and daily activities. TDEE is the starting point for setting up your diet – eating below TDEE leads to weight loss, eating above it leads to weight gain.

What is BMR?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the minimum number of calories your body needs to sustain basic life functions – breathing, heartbeat, organ function – at complete rest. BMR depends on your gender, age, height and weight. It is the foundation of calorie calculations and the basis for determining your TDEE.

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor formula?

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is a mathematical equation developed in 1990 used to calculate BMR. It is considered one of the most accurate formulas for the general population and is recommended by the American Dietetic Association. It takes gender, age, height and weight into account:

Men: BMR = (10 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (5 × age) + 5 Women: BMR = (10 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (5 × age) − 161

The result is then multiplied by a physical activity coefficient to obtain TDEE.

How to lose weight?

The foundation of weight loss is a calorie deficit – you need to consume fewer calories than your TDEE. A safe deficit is 300–500 kcal per day, which translates to losing around 0.3–0.5 kg per week. Key principles of effective weight loss:

  • Eat high protein (1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight) – protects muscle and keeps you full
  • Don't skip meals – regularity helps control hunger
  • Avoid drastic deficits (over 1000 kcal) – they slow down your metabolism
  • Combine diet with strength training – you will preserve lean muscle mass
  • Sleep 7–9 hours – lack of sleep increases appetite and makes fat loss harder
How to gain weight?

To gain weight, you need to consume more calories than your TDEE – this is called a calorie surplus. A recommended surplus is 200–400 kcal per day, which allows you to build muscle mass with minimal fat gain. Key principles:

  • Eat regular, calorie-dense meals – 4–6 times a day
  • High protein (1.6–2.2 g per kg) – essential for muscle growth
  • Train with weights – without strength training the surplus will turn into fat, not muscle
  • Choose calorie-dense, nutritious foods – nuts, avocado, rice, meat
  • Be patient – building muscle is a process measured in months, not weeks
